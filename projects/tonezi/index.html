ToneZi

Python, Flask, PostgreSQL, JavaScript, D3.js, Praat, Docker

ToneZi is a Mandarin learning web app built by a research team at Georgia Tech. Dr. Wu Hongchen leads it, with students Reed Jorgensen, Jim Fredeman, Mark Pindur, and me. It targets two skills most learning apps skip: writing a character from memory after hearing it, and producing the right tone contour when speaking.

The project grew out of the lab's research on character amnesia (提笔忘字, forgetting how to write characters you can read), presented at UN Chinese Language Learning Day 2024. ToneZi was deployed on DigitalOcean and used by two Chinese courses at Georgia Tech.

Writing

The Hanzi page plays a character's audio first and has the learner write it from memory. Characters come from the original research list, HSK 2.0, HSK 3.0, or a chengyu (four-character idiom) list, with an optional HSK level filter. Writing runs on HanziWriter, which checks stroke order as you draw, and every attempt's stroke data is saved for research. Pinyin, a definition, and an example sentence stay hidden until you ask for them.

Speaking

On the Pronunciation page, a learner types a phrase (or speaks freely) and records it in the browser. The server pulls the pitch curve from the recording and from a reference recording using Praat (through Parselmouth), tuned for the Mandarin pitch range of 75–500 Hz with smoothing to reduce octave errors. The two curves are plotted together in D3.js so the learner can see where their tones go wrong.

Reference audio comes from text-to-speech: Fish Audio, with Google TTS as an alternative. With voice cloning turned on, learners can record voice samples and hear the reference in a clone of their own voice, so the only difference between the two curves is the tone, not the speaker.

Architecture

The back end is a Flask app split into blueprints (writing, pronunciation, voice recording, voice cloning, accounts, feedback) on top of PostgreSQL, using a connection pool with retry and backoff. Recordings go through a hybrid audio cache: a file-based session cache for fast reads, written to the database in parallel. Slow or periodic work runs on a priority task queue with a memory-capped worker pool, so it never blocks a request on the small DigitalOcean server. Everything ships as a Docker image. The site also has a Chinese/English language toggle, accounts, a cookie consent flow, and a privacy page.

My work

  • Voice cloning. I integrated Fish Audio and then ElevenLabs voice cloning. The ElevenLabs plan allows only 10 voices, so I tracked every clone in the database and reused a returning learner's voice. When the slots filled up, the least recently used voices were evicted down to 8. A background job deletes clones older than an hour every 15 minutes, so slots free up without anyone cleaning them by hand.
  • Audio storage. I built the database layer that stores learner recordings and their metadata for research.
  • Privacy and UI. I added the cookie consent flow (stored in the database), the privacy page, error notifications, and UI work across the pronunciation, writing, and recording pages.
