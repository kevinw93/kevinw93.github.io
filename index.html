This fall I'm interning at Amazon Web Services on Journal, the distributed log that S3 and DynamoDB use to durably record writes before they're committed. My project is single-host failure detection for the stream router: noticing when one machine goes bad and quarantining it before it can stall writes for everyone else.

Over the summer I was at Lockheed Martin, building a search engine to replace the communications org's Algolia setup. It runs on OpenSearch inside Lockheed's own network, and leans on a knowledge graph built from thousands of internal documents to find results even when a query is worded differently from the page it's looking for.

Before that, I spent a summer at System Technology Works teaching a humanoid robot to see, talk, walk, and occasionally dance.

  • Modern Languages Lab, with Dr. Hongchen Wu2025–26

    Co-authored a paper on how well LLMs reason about unfamiliar languages, using 200+ olympiad problems. An outgrowth of competing in NACLO (gold) and at IOL on the US team (honorable mention).

At the Robotarium at Georgia Tech, I worked on getting the lab's small robots to find and follow each other using nothing but their onboard cameras, which mostly meant shrinking an object detector until it could run in real time on a Raspberry Pi.

I got into computational linguistics through linguistics olympiads: puzzles where you work out how a language you've never seen works from just a handful of examples. I won a gold medal at NACLO and went on to compete for the US at the International Linguistics Olympiad, where I received an honorable mention. So at the Modern Languages Lab with Dr. Hongchen Wu, it felt natural to turn those same puzzles on large language models. I co-authored a paper testing how well LLMs reason about unfamiliar languages across more than two hundred olympiad problems.
